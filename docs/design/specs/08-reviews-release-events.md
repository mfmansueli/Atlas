# From editing to the App Store, and what people say back

Three additions that close the loop Atlas already starts: prepare the listing in every language, submit the version, then read and answer what people write, in their language.

**On the canvas** (page *Avaliações, envio e eventos*): **Prototype** starts on Overview with Version 1.4. Try Submit for Review → accept Swedish → answer encryption → Submit; then Reviews (⌘8) → Send Reply or Reply to All 38; then In-App Events (⌘5): open an event, **Make the Artwork…**, click a concept, **View Full Size**. Boards **38–40** show every part and state, light and dark, plus the minimum window; board **47** is the artwork.

## Ratings & Reviews

- **Translated by default**, with *Show Original* on every review. A switch in the header turns translation off.
- **Suggested reply** in the reviewer’s language, marked with its source (Claude Code), with the English meaning underneath. Send Reply, Edit…, or Dismiss. Replies are public: the card says so before you send.
- **After sending**: “You replied Nov 16. Apple shows it under the review, usually within a day.” Edit stays available; Undo in the toast.
- **What people write about**: reviews grouped by subject, each with a count, the countries, and one action: reply to all of them, open Pricing for Japan, show the reviews, or suggest words for the store page.
- **Reply to All**: one message per language, each sent to its own review. Reviews you already answered are left out.
- **Ratings**: Apple’s average and count for every country and for the current version. The bars count written reviews only, and say so.

## Submit for Review and release

The Version card lists what goes to App Review and what’s missing, then follows the version: Submitted → Waiting for Review → In Review → Approved → On the App Store.

- **Build**: ready builds to choose from; one still processing is shown, disabled.
- **What’s New** in every language. A missing language blocks Submit and is suggested in place.
- **Goes with this submission**: accepted changes not pushed yet (“Push and Submit for Review” does both), the In-App Event that goes with the version, and the checks that already pass.
- **Encryption**: asked once, saved to App Information → Export Compliance.
- **After approval**: right away, by you, or on a date; phased release over 7 days; reset the rating summary, off by default with the reason to turn it on.
- Submit stays disabled until everything is there, and the footer says exactly what’s missing.

| Status | What the card offers |
| --- | --- |
| Waiting for Review | How long it usually takes · Cancel Submission |
| Approved, waiting for you | Release 1.4 Now |
| Phased release | Day and percentage · Pause or Resume · Release to Everyone Now |
| Rejected | Apple’s message in full · Edit App Review Information · Reply in App Store Connect · Submit Again |

TestFlight, builds and the conversation with App Review stay in Xcode and App Store Connect, and the card says so.

## In-App Events

Redesigned on November 18: a gallery of events, then one event in a single column. Before, the app’s sidebar, the list of events, the editor and the preview sat side by side, and the editor was squeezed between them. Board 40.

### The gallery

- Every event is drawn as its App Store card: artwork, badge, name and short description. Under it: the reference name, status (Draft, Goes with 1.4, Ended), dates, and one line that says what needs you.
- **New Event…** and **Ask Claude Code…** are in the header.
- Cards are at least 270 pt wide and fill the row: three across at 1200, two at the minimum window.

### One event

- **Header:** Events (back), the event’s name as a menu that switches to another event, status, Ask Claude Code…, and the event’s one action (Submit Event…, Open Submission, Duplicate Event).
- **First block, On the App Store:** the event as people see it, with the text of the language being edited. A switch shows it as the **Event Card** or as **Event Details**. The artwork sits beside it. Under 700 pt of width the two stack.
- **Then one column,** at most 780 pt wide: the language bar, Event Name (30), Short Description (50), Long Description (120), results for an ended event, and the event’s settings.
- Suggestions explain themselves. In Brazil December is summer, so “Winter Reflection Challenge” becomes “Desafio de Fim de Ano”.
- Until a language is accepted, the page says people there see English; the preview shows the suggestion and says so.
- Arabic and other right-to-left languages run right to left in the fields and on the card.
- Ended events show Apple’s numbers: event page views, opens and downloads from the event.

### What was traded

- Going to another event used to be one click in the list. Now it is the menu on the event’s name, or Events.
- The preview scrolls away with the page. The name and the short description, the two texts on the card, are in view with it at 1200 × 780.

## In-App Events · artwork from Claude Design

Every event needs two images, and they are the first thing people see. Atlas writes the brief from the event, Claude Design draws three concepts on the event itself, and any of them opens at full size. Board 47.

| Image | Size | Where it shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Event card | 1920 × 1080 (16:9) | Search, Today and the product page |
| Event details | 1080 × 1920 (9:16) | The top of the event’s page |

Each can also be a video of up to 30 seconds, and each can differ per language.

### The flow

| Moment | Where | What the person sees |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nothing yet | The event | The card it has and an amber slot for the missing details image. **Make the Artwork…** and **Choose Files…** |
| The brief | A sheet, the only one | A brief already written from the event’s name and descriptions, editable. What Claude Design gets: the event, the app’s design system, and (a switch) a capture of the screen the event is about. Two options: words in the art, and a moving version. The look: a new look from Claude Design, a template, or extend the card you have. One sentence at the bottom says what will be made and how long it takes. |
| Drawing | The event | The sheet closes at once. Three rows fill in as each concept is done, with one progress bar. A concept that is ready can already be clicked and opened. The menu bar icon shows Atlas is working. |
| Choosing | The event | Three rows: the pair as thumbnails, the name, one line of reasoning, and Claude Design’s pick marked. Clicking a row shows that concept in the preview, on the real card, in the language being edited. Nothing is chosen until **Use This Artwork**. |
| Looking closely | The viewer | See below. |
| Chosen | The event | The pair, who made it, **View Full Size** and **Change…**, which brings the three concepts back with the one in use marked. |

### The viewer

- **Opens from** View Full Size, the arrows on a concept’s row, the preview itself, or a thumbnail. It covers the whole window, on black in both appearances.
- **Top:** close, the concept’s name and “Concept 1 of 3”, Event Card or Event Details, **App Store Text** (the badge, name and description the App Store puts over the image), **Text Area** (hatches where that text sits), and full screen.
- **Sides and bottom:** arrows and a strip of thumbnails to move between concepts; the reasoning and the checks; the language of the text; **Ask for Changes…** and **Use This Artwork**.
- **Ask for Changes…** takes a sentence, or a tap on a suggestion, and redraws only that pair. The image stays in view while it changes.
- **Full screen** leaves only the image. A small bar with previous, next, Use This Artwork and Exit Full Screen shows when the pointer moves and goes away after two seconds.
- **Keys:** Space opens the viewer on the concept shown, ← and → move, esc leaves full screen and then closes, ⌃⌘F is full screen. They are in the tooltips; the prototype doesn’t answer to them.
- The image is as large as fits: its width is the smaller of the stage’s width and the stage’s height times the ratio, so nothing is cropped at any window size.

### Rules

- **No words in the art by default.** The App Store writes the name and description over the image in each language, so one pair works everywhere. With words on, Atlas makes a pair per language and says so before it starts.
- **The lower third stays calm.** The brief asks for it and Text Area shows it, because that is where Apple’s text sits.
- **Card and details always come as a pair**, from one composition. “Extend the card you have” is the exception: it keeps your card and makes only the details image.
- **Looking is not choosing.** The preview and the viewer can show any concept; only Use This Artwork puts a pair in Review & Push, and its message has Undo.
- **Nothing reaches the App Store without you.** Submit Event… stays off until both images exist and every language is written or accepted.
- **Free and Pro.** Choose Files… and the viewer are free. Make the Artwork… is Pro and opens the paywall on the free plan.
- **No design system connected:** the first look option is off and templates are the default, the same as for screenshots.

### Not drawn yet

- **Autopilot.** When a version ships with an event that has no art, Autopilot’s “What to make” step should offer a fourth card, Event artwork, so it is made in the same run as the screenshots. Described here, not on a board.
- **The template picker** and the check after **Choose Files…**. The second can be the viewer with Text Area on.
- **The moving version’s player.** The viewer marks it (▶ 0:12) and the art twinkles in the prototype; scrubbing and a poster-frame picker are not designed.
- **The earlier version** of a concept after Ask for Changes…. The message says it is kept; where to get it back is not drawn.

**To confirm before building:** the exact area Apple’s text covers on each image (the guide here is the lower third, drawn by eye), and uploading event images and video through `appEventScreenshots` and `appEventVideoClips`, which I have not tested. The three concepts on the board are stand-ins made of shapes; Claude Design draws the real ones.

## App Compliance

A new page in **App Information**, after General, matching Apple’s new App Compliance section. Board **44**.

| Regulation | What Atlas fills in | What stays with you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Digital Services Act (EU) | Suggests **trader** when the app sells anything in the EU, with name, address, phone and email prepared from App Store Connect’s business details and the support URL. | The decision. “I’m a Trader” or “Stay a Non-Trader”. Apple verifies the phone and email with a code after the push. |
| China mainland ICP Filing Number | Notices the app is sold in China mainland without a number, and checks the number’s format before pushing. | The number itself (only China’s MIIT issues it), or removing China mainland from availability. |
| Vietnam Game License | Checks the category: not a game, so not needed. | For games sold in Vietnam: the license. |
| Regulated Medical Devices | Checks the category and the age-rating answer for Medical or Treatment Information: not needed for Lumen. | For Medical or Health & Fitness apps: the declaration. |

- **Never accepted for you.** Compliance answers are legal statements, so they are left out of Accept All Pages and “accept right away”. The page says so at the top.
- The page’s status in the list reads “2 need you” in the warning color, not as purple suggestions, and “Up to date” once everything is answered or checked.
- Checks run again when the category, age rating or availability changes.
- The preview shows the trader details as they’ll appear on the page in the EU.

## App Store Connect API

| Part | Resources |
| --- | --- |
| Reviews | `customerReviews` per app and territory · `customerReviewResponses` to create, replace or delete a reply |
| Ratings summary | Apple’s public lookup per storefront (average and count, all versions and current version) |
| Submission | `reviewSubmissions` and `reviewSubmissionItems` (version and In-App Events together) · `appStoreVersions` (build, release type, earliest release date) · `appStoreVersionLocalizations` (What’s New) |
| Release | `appStoreVersionPhasedReleases` (start, pause, resume, complete) · `appStoreVersionReleaseRequests` (release a version waiting for you) |
| Events | `appEvents`, `appEventLocalizations`, `appEventScreenshots`, `appEventVideoClips` |
