What Atlas does

Your App Store page, right in every country: words, screenshots, prices.

It reads your apps, shows them as the App Store does, and suggests improvements in every language.

  • It suggests. Suggestions come from your assistant and Atlas’s own checks. They’re always purple.
  • You review. Accept, edit or dismiss each one, or a whole language at once.
  • One push sends it. Either way, nothing is sent until you push in Review & Push.
Atlas doesn’t replace Xcode or TestFlight: builds, testers and App Review stay where they are.

Connect App Store Connect

Atlas uses an App Store Connect API key to read your apps and to push what you approve.

Make the key

  1. In App Store Connect, open Users and Access, then Integrations.
  2. Choose App Store Connect API, then Team Keys, and add a key.
  3. Give it the Admin role, or App Manager if you don’t need sales figures.
  4. Download the .p8 file. Apple lets you download it once.

Give it to Atlas

  1. Copy the Issuer ID from the top of that page and the Key ID from the key’s row.
  2. In Atlas, paste both and drop the .p8 file on the window.
  3. Click Connect. Atlas reads your apps and changes nothing.
The key stays in Keychain. Requests are signed here and go straight to Apple.

How suggestions work

Purple means a suggestion: not accepted, and not on the App Store.

  • A purple number in the sidebar counts a section’s waiting suggestions.
  • Each suggestion sits under its field and says who made it and why.
  • Accept keeps it, Edit lets you change it first, Dismiss removes it.
  • Accept All Languages takes every language at once; untick any in the list first.
You accept prices, App Privacy answers (not its URLs), the age rating and App Compliance one by one.

Review &amp; Push

What you accept or edit waits in Review & Push. One push sends it to App Store Connect.

  1. Click Review & Push at the top right. The number counts waiting changes.
  2. Read the changes by section and language. Untick anything that should wait.
  3. Click Push. Atlas shows each change as it lands.

If a push stops halfway, what landed stays on the App Store; the rest stays in Review & Push.

Some changes go live at once, like promotional text and prices. Others wait for your next version’s review. Review & Push marks which.

Store Page in every language

The name, subtitle, description, keywords and What’s New, for each language you sell in.

  • Use the language menu at the top to switch. A dot marks languages with suggestions.
  • The preview on the right shows the page as people there will see it.
  • A counter on each field shows Apple’s limit. Text over the limit can’t be pushed.

To add a language, choose Add Language… in the language menu. Atlas suggests the whole page from your source language.

Screenshots and Autopilot

Screenshots for every device and language, from templates or a Claude Design look.

Each set is a template, your app’s screens and a caption per language. Edit the caption and every device follows.

Autopilot

  1. Say what to make: screenshots, an App Preview, header artwork.
  2. Choose the look.
  3. Check the plan and start. Atlas captures, designs and writes every language.
  4. Come back twice: to choose the look, and to review and push.
Autopilot needs Xcode on this Mac and an assistant connected. It is part of Atlas Pro.

Product pages for an audience

Other versions of your App Store page, each with its own link, for a campaign, an audience or a search.

A page can change screenshots, App Previews and promotional text per language. Name, icon and description match your main page.

  1. Open Custom Product Pages and click New Page… Name it, and say who it is for.
  2. Per language: write promotional text, choose keywords, keep or replace the screenshots.
  3. Click Push and Submit… Atlas pushes the page, then you submit it to App Review.
  • Keywords come from your last approved version. People searching them see this page once it’s approved and visible.
  • A deep link sends people who already have the app to the right place in it.
  • Hiding a page needs no review. Its link then opens your main page.
Up to 70 pages, each reviewed alone, with no new app version.

In-App Events and their artwork

App Store cards for what’s happening in your app, in every language.

An event needs a name, two descriptions, dates and two images: the card (1920 × 1080) and details image (1080 × 1920).

Make the artwork

  1. Open the event and click Make the Artwork…
  2. Check Atlas’s brief and choose the look. Concepts appear on the event while you keep working.
  3. Click a concept to see it on the App Store card. Each includes the card and details image.
  4. Click View Full Size to see it large, with the App Store’s text, in any language. The top-right arrows make it full screen.
  5. Click Use This Artwork.
Leave words out of the art. The App Store adds the event’s name in each language.

App Information, privacy and compliance

What Apple asks once per app: category, age rating, accessibility, App Privacy, App Compliance.

  • Each page shows its status: up to date, suggestions waiting, or needs you.
  • An assistant can read your project and suggest answers, with evidence beside each.
  • App Compliance covers rules in some countries: trader details in the EU, the ICP filing number in China mainland, and licences.
These are your statements to Apple and regulators. Atlas never accepts them for you, even with Accept All Pages.

Pricing by country

What your subscriptions and purchases cost in 175 countries and regions.

  • Keep my prices shows what you charge and changes nothing.
  • Show suggestions suggests a price per country from local buying power and your sales.
  • Raising a price people already pay asks them to agree. Atlas shows how many first.
Setting prices yourself is free. Suggested prices for every country are part of Atlas Pro.

Submit for Review and release

Send a version to App Review from Overview and follow it to the App Store.

  1. On Overview, the version card lists what goes to Apple and what is missing.
  2. Click Submit for Review… Choose the build, check What’s New in every language, and answer the encryption question.
  3. Choose the release after approval: right away, manually, or on a date.

The sidebar’s bottom shows the version’s status. If Apple rejects it, the card shows Apple’s full message and where to fix it.

Reply to reviews

Every review, translated, with a suggested reply in the reviewer’s language.

  • Reviews are shown in your language. Show Original shows what the person wrote.
  • A suggested reply shows its meaning in your language underneath, so you know what you send.
  • Reply to All answers every review about one subject, with one message per language.
Replies are public. Apple shows them under the review, usually within a day. You can edit them later.

Analytics

Where people find your app, where they give up, and what to change.

  1. Click Ask Apple for Reports. The first time, Apple takes a day or two.
  2. Read the funnel: impressions, page views, downloads, then trials and paid starts.
  3. Use Break It Down to compare countries and sources, and What to Try for changes worth making.

For trials, paid starts and proceeds, add your vendor number in Settings → Accounts. The key needs Admin, Finance or Sales.

Connect an assistant

Atlas charges nothing for usage. Suggestions come from your assistant, API key or Atlas’s checks.

  1. Open Settings → AI Assistants and turn on Let assistants connect.
  2. Click Pair Assistant… and copy the command for your assistant.
  3. Run it in your project’s folder. The assistant appears under Paired assistants.

Your key and your data

Your App Store Connect key never leaves this Mac.

  • The key stays in Keychain. Requests are signed here and go straight to Apple.
  • An assistant gets only what it asks for through the local server: text, captures, prices. Never a key.
  • With an API key, suggested text goes from this Mac to your chosen provider.
  • Atlas’s developer receives nothing unless you turn on sharing in Settings → Privacy & Data.

Fix a problem

The few things that go wrong, and what to do.

The key was refused

The key was revoked, or its IDs don’t match the .p8 file. Make a new key and add it in Settings → Accounts.

Atlas can’t read sales

The key’s role is App Manager or Developer. Sales, trials and proceeds need Admin, Finance or Sales.

A push stopped halfway

What landed stays on the App Store. The rest waits in Review & Push with Apple’s reason. Fix it and push again.

Apple can’t be reached

Your edits stay on this Mac, and Atlas retries when you’re back online. Check Apple’s status from the Help menu.

An assistant won’t connect

Check that Let assistants connect is on and that Atlas is running. If you made a new token, pair the assistant again.
